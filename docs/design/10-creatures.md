## 10. Creatures and civilizations

The underground is home to several small species, each living in colonies that run their own simulation. Every creature is an individual with a name, a role, needs and memories, like dwarves in Dwarf Fortress. They are actors in their own stories, and that is where most emergent stories come from.

### Species are generated from archetypes

Each world picks 4–6 species from an archetype pool and randomizes their culture. An archetype fixes body plan, home layer and diet; the culture roll decides the rest.

| Attribute   | What it decides                     | Example values                                             |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Home layer  | Where colonies appear               | Stone, Crystal caverns, Fungal deep…                       |
| Treasures   | Materials they value highly         | Dirt, gold, glowcaps, obsidian                             |
| Disdains    | Materials they think are worthless  | Often a common material the player will want               |
| Tradition   | The technique only they know        | Glassblowing, brewing, rune-binding, beast-taming          |
| Temperament | How they react to strangers         | Shy, curious, proud, wary                                  |
| Light       | Whether they like or avoid light    | Light-lovers welcome torches; dark-dwellers want them gone |
| Healing     | Whether members can heal with magic | At least 2 species per world must have healers             |
| Rhythm      | When they sleep, forage and trade   | Affects when you can find them home                        |

**Illustrative archetypes** (names and details are placeholders): Mosslings (shy fungal-layer foragers, brewers), Cinderfolk (proud smiths at the magma edge who treasure water), Glimmerkin (light-loving crystal dwellers who strangely prize plain dirt), Burrowers (nomadic traders who carry news between colonies).

### Individuals

Every creature stores: name, species, role (elder, healer, crafter, forager, scout, child), two personality traits, current needs, home colony, relationships with other creatures, and a memory list of notable events involving the player (gifts, rescues, harm).

Creatures move through the world, including through tunnels the player dug. Scouts and foragers travel on errands, and errands can lead them into danger. That is where rescue opportunities come from.

### Colony simulation

Each colony tracks: population, a stockpile of materials and food, needs and how well they're met, mood, structures, threats, and relations with other colonies.

Each tick, a colony consumes food and materials, produces goods from its tradition, trades with neighbors it is connected to, and grows or shrinks based on needs met. Far from the player this runs as cheap abstract math; near the player its members act it out.

### Problems come from the simulation

Colony problems are never scripted quests. They emerge from the same rules as everything else, including the player's own actions.

- **Flooding:** an aquifer breaches, often because the player dug nearby.
- **Depletion:** the material their tradition depends on runs out.
- **Predators:** a predator population grows near their foraging routes.
- **Collapse:** a cave-in blocks their tunnels or damages homes.
- **Blight:** spreading fungus or spore gas reaches their cavern.
- **Heat surge:** a lava flow shifts toward them.
- **Rivalry:** two growing colonies compete for the same resource.

The colony shows its problem through icons and behavior (worried animations, members gathering at the trouble spot). The player solves it with the normal verbs: digging, building, crafting and giving.

### Communication

Creatures communicate in picture icons and gestures. As the player's bond with a species grows, they learn its words and short phrases start to appear (Could). Each species should feel culturally distinct through what it asks for, how it greets you and what it celebrates.
