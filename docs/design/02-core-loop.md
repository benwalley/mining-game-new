## 2. Core loop and sessions

The game runs on four nested loops. Each one should feel complete on its own, so a player can stop after any of them with something gained.

| Loop    | Length              | What happens                                                                     | What the player keeps                  |
| ------- | ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Dig     | Under 1 minute      | Dig a few tiles, find something: an ore, a cavern, a critter, a hazard           | A material, a discovery                |
| Trip    | 2–5 minutes         | Head down, fill the pack, come back to camp or a colony to smelt, craft or trade | A new item, a trade, a bond step       |
| Project | 15 minutes to hours | Build an elevator, fix a colony's flood, forge an alloy, push to a new layer     | A structure, a technique, a new friend |
| World   | Weeks of play       | Pursue the world's big optional ambitions, unite colonies, reach the depths      | A world that feels like yours          |

### Session rules

- **Instant resume.** Opening the game drops the player exactly where they were, in under 2 seconds. No title screen on return, no menus.
- **Constant saving.** The game saves dirty chunks every few seconds and whenever the page is hidden or closed. Losing progress to a closed tab is a bug.
- **Natural stopping points.** A full pack, arriving at camp, and finishing a trade are all good places to stop. The game should never trap the player mid-action.
- **Weight defines the trip.** Pack capacity and item weight set how long a trip lasts before returning. Better packs, carts and elevators stretch it.
- **While you were gone (Should).** On return, distant colonies have progressed a little in the abstract simulation. A short summary card shows up to three notable changes. Absence must never punish the player: no colony collapses or rescues are missed while the game is closed.
