## 12. Danger, death and rescue

The player can die, but it should be uncommon and mostly an inconvenience. When the player falls, the nearest creature that can heal comes to save them, and that rescuer may be someone they have never met. Death is a way to meet people, not a fail state.

### Hazards

Every hazard is telegraphed at least 2 seconds before it can hurt, and every hazard has a crafted counter.

| Hazard                   | Warning signs                                       | Counter                                                               |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Heat (depth, lava, fire) | Orange tint, shimmer, sweating, crackle             | Heat suits, insulating walls, water, cooling teachings                |
| Lava                     | Glow and heat before contact                        | Water to make obsidian, walls, pumps                                  |
| Cave-in                  | Dust, creaking, cracks                              | Support beams, tougher materials                                      |
| Gas                      | Haze, hiss, coughing                                | Suits, sealing doors, venting, lighting it from far away if flammable |
| Drowning                 | Rising water sound, air indicator by breath bubbles | Pumps, walls, air teachings                                           |
| Falls                    | Long drops are visible                              | Ladders, ropes, platforms                                             |
| Predators                | Growls, tracks, scattered critters                  | Light, walls, avoiding their territory (combat is an open question)   |

Health regenerates slowly when the player is out of danger. Lethal outcomes need sustained exposure. A player who notices the warnings and retreats should almost always survive.

### What happens on death

1. The player collapses. The screen dims to a soft vignette; the world keeps running.
2. The game finds the nearest creature with healing magic who can reach the player, and sends them along a real path.
3. While they travel, the camera stays on the fallen player with a faint pulse. The wait is a time-lapse: in real time it lasts roughly 3–15 seconds, scaled by the rescuer's distance, while in-world time passes faster.
4. The rescuer arrives, heals the player with a visible magic effect, and carries them to their home.
5. The player wakes in the rescuer's home, beside them.

The cost of dying is the time spent, being moved away from where you were, and whatever changed in the world meanwhile (water flowed, a fire spread). The player keeps their pack. Whether death should cost anything more is an open question.

### Rescuers

- **Nearest healer wins.** If a friend is close, they come. If a stranger is closer, the stranger comes.
- **Strangers make first contact.** Waking up in an unknown colony is a guaranteed discovery and a memorable introduction to a species the player may not have known existed.
- **Wandering healers.** The generator places a few solitary healers near the most dangerous zones, so a rescuer always exists, even before the player has met anyone.
- **Repeated deaths.** If the same rescuer saves the player several times, they start to tease or scold (with icons), which adds personality and a gentle nudge to be careful.

### Life debts

Being rescued creates a **life debt** to that individual. It shows in the journal under People. The world then gives the player chances to repay it:

- The rescuer goes on errands like any creature, and errands sometimes lead into danger: trapped by a cave-in, cut off by water, cornered by a predator.
- The storyteller (section 15) nudges the timing so a repayment chance appears within a reasonable stretch of play, ideally when the player is nearby.
- The player sees a distress icon and can help with the normal verbs: dig them out, build a wall, drain the water.

Saving the rescuer clears the debt and raises the bond to **Kin**. They take the player home to meet their family, and the whole colony starts at Friend. This is story 2 in practice.
