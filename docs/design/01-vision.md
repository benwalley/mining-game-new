## 1. Vision and pillars

**Pitch:** You dig down through a living underground world, craft anything from what you find, and befriend the little civilizations hidden in its caverns. The controls fit one thumb. The world underneath is as deep as Dwarf Fortress.

There is no main quest and no win screen. Players set their own goals, and the world's systems create the stories. The game should be fun for two minutes on a bus or two hours on a couch.

### Design pillars

Every feature should serve at least one pillar. If it serves none, cut it.

1. **Simple hands, deep world.** One gesture to move and dig, one tap to act. All complexity lives in the simulation, never in the UI.
2. **Systems, not scripts.** Build a few consistent rules with almost no exceptions, and let them combine. Fun should come out of the system rather than being authored event by event.
3. **Kindness compounds.** Helping creatures is the most powerful strategy in the game. Generosity should feel like it pays off in surprising ways.
4. **Make anything from what you find.** Crafting is the center of the game. Item stats come from the materials used, so players can make things the designer never listed.
5. **Every world is its own story.** Each new world should feel different in ways that change how you play, not just where the rocks are.
6. **Two minutes or two hours.** Every session, however short, ends with progress. Long sessions have deep projects to sink into.

### What it is not

- Not a quest game. Nothing tells the player what to do next.
- Not graphics-heavy. Visuals stay simple so the CPU goes to simulation.
- Not online. Single player, fully local, works offline.
- Not punishing. Death is possible but uncommon, and mostly an inconvenience.
